# Gerber viewer

## Schematic editor

Open `/editor`, or choose **Schematic editor** from either viewer. The editor uses `OrthographicViewer2D`, which reuses the shared viewport, rulers, grid, pan, zoom, and fit controls with a locked orthographic camera. The sheet border is landscape A3, 420 × 297 mm, with the origin at its lower-left corner.

The sheet has a KiCad-style double frame, numbered columns, lettered rows, and a bottom-right title block. Outside the physical sheet edge, the grid uses 30% of its inside opacity; the boundary follows sheet size, pan, and zoom. `Editor2D` accepts a `sheet` prop with `width` and `height` in millimetres and an optional `name`, for example `<Editor2D sheet={{ width: 210, height: 297, name: "A4" }} />`. Changing these values rebuilds the worksheet and fit bounds without changing the drawing objects. Zones divide the available width and height evenly; margins, coordinate bands, and the title block shrink to fit small sheets. Custom sizes display their dimensions when no name is supplied. The title, file, date, and revision fields are blank until document metadata is added.

The bottom toolbar groups related tools behind one button each. Open **Shapes** for Rectangle, Circle, Triangle, Line, and Text, **Connections** for Wire and Pin, **History tools** for Undo, Redo, and History, or **View tools** for zoom, fit, and PNG export. Each group opens a secondary toolbar above the main row. Opening another group replaces it. Click outside or press Escape to close it. Arrow keys move between secondary toolbar buttons. Select, Pan, Symbols, and Delete stay in the main toolbar. On narrow screens, scroll the main toolbar horizontally to reach every button.

The drawing fills the screen, with navigation links at the upper left and the attributes panel floating at the upper right, as in the Gerber viewer. Choose Rectangle, Circle, Triangle, Wire, or Pin from the bottom floating toolbar, then drag on the sheet to draw a shape. Rectangles and triangles use two opposite corners. For a circle, drag from its center to set the radius; choose Pin and click to place a single connection point. Wires must start at a pin (including symbol pins) or an open wire endpoint. Click to add horizontal or vertical segments; click another pin or open wire endpoint to connect and finish. Escape or Enter ends a chain while retaining its placed segments, leaving an open endpoint that another wire can start or finish at. Connection points snap exactly, even off the grid; finishing at an offset terminal inserts a right-angle bend. A preview and its dimensions update while dragging. Release to finish, or press Escape to cancel. A click without a drag creates nothing for rectangles, triangles, and circles. The same toolbar contains Select, Pan, Delete, zoom, and fit controls. Finishing a shape returns to Select. Drag a shape to move it. The A3 border is a paper guide; shapes can be drawn and moved across it or entirely outside it. The most recently placed shape wins when shapes overlap. Drag empty space to select every shape touched by a selection rectangle. Shift + drag adds shapes to the selection; Shift + click toggles a shape. Drag a selected shape to move the group, preserving the spacing between shapes. Use Pan or middle-drag to move the view. Ctrl + scroll or pinch zooms, and F6 fits the sheet. Selecting a shape opens an attributes drawer with its type, ID, center position, and dimensions. Attribute edits apply when you leave the field, or press Enter in a single-line field, and create one undo step for the completed value. Multiple selections show their count and overall bounds. Clearing the selection closes the drawer. With the drawing focused, arrow keys move the selected shapes by one grid step, Shift + arrows by ten, ordinary text by a tenth of a step, and Delete or Backspace removes the selection. Escape cancels a drag or clears selection. Each shape retains its drawn dimensions and remains in memory for the current page session.

Use the **Symbols** toolbar button to open the symbol library. Choose **Resistor**, **Capacitor**, or **Ground**, move the preview to the desired location, and click to place it; Escape cancels placement. Each built-in symbol fits a 6 mm overall span, including its terminal markers, with proportions referenced from KiCad and 0.254 mm strokes. Symbols snap to the grid by their center. Clicking any child shape or touching it with a selection rectangle selects the entire symbol. Drag the symbol body to move the whole symbol. Drag an attached label to change only its position on that instance. Arrow keys and Delete act on the whole selected symbol, including in mixed selections with ordinary shapes. The Attributes panel edits each instance's name, reference, value, center, angle in 90° increments, and line width. It also edits each label's X and Y position relative to the symbol before rotation. Label positions stay with their instance when saving and reopening the drawing. Each instance displays its reference and value as attached text; editing either attribute updates the label. Symbol children stay together without individual resize handles. Insertion, movement, deletion, and attribute changes support undo/redo.

The grid has 10 × 10 small cells per major square. Ruler labels remain at the major intervals; snapping is always on and follows the finer cells as zoom changes. The editor has no snap toggle.

`packages/core/src/components/editor` owns the shapes and their graphics resources. Symbol instances own local child geometry and independent attributes; built-in definitions live in `symbols.ts`. Symbol children are excluded from standalone wire merging and junction movement. Edits update the existing scene without resetting the camera. `OrthographicViewer2D` also accepts a general `ViewerScene` and the same reset, zoom, and pan handle as `Viewer2D`.

## Viewing Gerber files

Open Gerber files or ZIP archives to view their planar objects in the shared perspective viewport. Each Gerber file in an archive, including subfolders, becomes a separate layer, just as if it had been selected individually. Archive paths identify the layers; unrelated files and hidden metadata are skipped. Archives and individual files can be selected together and are added to the existing layers. Invalid files produce errors without discarding successful imports. A file with recoverable problems still renders everything that could be read. Its row in the layer panel shows how many errors and warnings it has; select the count to list each problem with its line number. In a project, which has no layer panel, the message banner lists them. Files are parsed in browser workers. A Gerber file larger than 64 MiB, or a ZIP archive larger than 256 MiB, is rejected before it is read; ZIP archives skip entries over 64 MiB. Files rejected this way are not added to the project. Layer visibility, ordering, removal, and focus remain available; focusing a layer shows the others as cool blue X-ray outlines with faint interiors. Overlapping unfocused layers accumulate light, while clear holes stay transparent. The viewport requires WebGL 2 and reports an error if it cannot initialize.

The Gerber adapter in `packages/core/src/formats/gerber` targets Ucamco's [2026.05 specification](https://www.ucamco.com/en/guest/downloads/gerber-format), including X3 component attributes. Unsupported commands and malformed input produce diagnostics with one of three severities. A warning means the file was read as written or repaired: legacy syntax, a missing `M02`, or a region contour that needed a closing segment. An error means one command was skipped, such as an unsupported G-code or a rectangular-aperture arc stroke, so the layer may be missing that geometry. A fatal problem makes the whole image wrong or unreadable and rejects the layer with its reason: legacy negative images, nonidentity image transforms, missing units or coordinate format, and files over the size or object limits.

`packages/core/src/engine/types.ts` defines the shared geometry in millimetres. The low-level Gerber parser returns vector geometry in `ParseResult.object`, alongside diagnostics, format, and units. The file parser resolves exposures in a worker and constructs the Three.js object before returning. The WebGL viewport in `packages/core/src/engine/scene-viewport.ts` displays that object without importing a file parser. To add another format, implement `FormatParser` and register it in `packages/core/src/formats/registry.ts`. Gerber attributes remain on scene objects.

Run `pnpm run dev` for the web app or `pnpm run dev:desktop /absolute/symbol-directory` for the desktop app. Run `pnpm test` for parser and renderer geometry tests. Both apps use the same editor and viewer components. See [symbol files](symbol-format.md) for browser imports and desktop directory storage.

Gerber uses `Viewer2D`, a perspective camera locked above the scene looking down the Z axis. Drag or swipe to pan, pinch or Ctrl + scroll to zoom, and use `F6` to fit. Mouse modifiers and touch gestures cannot rotate the camera. Gerber and STEP share the zoom and fit toolbar; STEP additionally exposes isometric, top, and front views. Visibility, focus, and reordering update the displayed objects without rebuilding geometry or resetting the camera. X-ray outlines combine a camera-relative width of 4 CSS pixels with a scene-relative width of 0.05 mm, both extending inward from each edge. The wider contour remains visible without doubling the brightness where the two overlap. Perspective scales the scene-relative contour with edge distance, while the camera-relative contour keeps distant edges visible.

Gerber objects remain flat surfaces, separated by a 0.1 mm display gap along Z. The bottom entry in the layer panel is at Z = 0; higher entries sit progressively higher. Reordering updates these positions, hiding preserves the gaps, and removing a layer closes its gap. This spacing does not represent physical PCB thickness. Camera fitting includes the full stack. Each file is a Three.js object containing triangle meshes and contour geometry. Filled curves are tessellated with a 0.0001 mm tolerance. X-ray contours use cached levels of detail selected before rendering, with simplification limited to 0.25 CSS pixels and 0.005 mm. Zooming in restores the finer contour geometry. Flat fills render in a single pass. There are no texture maps or backing images. Net hover selection is not implemented in the shared perspective viewport.

## Compatible 2D and 3D scene inputs

Both `Viewer2D` and `Viewer3D` accept the same `scene: ViewerScene` prop, defined in `packages/core/src/engine/scene.ts`. A scene is an ordered array of `ViewerObject` entries. Each entry is either a `PlanarObject` (vector primitives, bounds, and display settings) or a `SpatialObject` (a Three.js `Object3D`). There are no separate layer and model slots; any number of either object type can appear in the array. The same scene can be shown in both viewers at once.

```tsx
const step = await parseStepFile(stepFile);
const gerber = await parseGerberFile(gerberFile);
const scene = [...step.scene, ...gerber.scene];
<Viewer2D scene={scene} />
<Viewer3D scene={scene} />
```

STEP files larger than 256 MiB are rejected before they are read. `parseStepFile` constructs one spatial object per part before resolving and returns the scene, part names and triangle counts, and an idempotent `dispose()` method. Raw importer meshes stay inside the parsing implementation. `parseGerberFile` returns a spatial scene object containing the completed mesh group as `result.object` and `result.scene[0]`, with an idempotent `dispose()` method. Clear exposures are subtracted from earlier dark geometry; later dark exposures can add geometry back. Aperture holes stay local to their shapes. Workspaces can combine parsed objects and change their display colors without rebuilding their geometry.

Both viewers use the same perspective camera, scene display, rendering, navigation code, and grid beneath the geometry. `Viewer2D` locks rotation to the top view and keeps pan and zoom available; `Viewer3D` allows orbiting. Both planar and solid inputs become actual Three.js mesh objects; planar data supplied directly is triangulated by the shared scene builder. No separate orthographic or vector viewport is used by `Viewer2D`.

Top views draw the grid dots in the WebGL canvas, beneath the scene, as one point per dot in view. The paper sheet is a plain layer behind the canvas. The rulers lay out their ticks one viewport past each edge and slide them with the view, so most pan frames repaint no DOM. They lay the ticks out again near the end of that range, or once the view rests at a fraction of a pixel, where labels would look soft.

While the orthographic editor view only moves, each frame shifts the previous picture by whole device pixels and draws only the strips that scroll into view. Subtrees outside a strip are hidden for that draw. A change to the scene, the zoom or the canvas size redraws everything, as does hover in a viewer with picking. Gerber views always redraw, because their outlines adjust to the zoom. The picture sits at the nearest whole pixel, at most half a pixel from the controls' camera, and later redraws keep that position until the camera moves.

Viewers borrow input geometry and materials. Each viewer owns its display materials, renderer, controls, and grid; parsed geometry remains owned by the caller. Close every viewer using a parsed scene before disposing it. The file hooks release removed or replaced objects and clean up on unmount. `useFileImport` releases disposable results from cancelled imports.

`StepViewer` applies part visibility and wireframe controls to the parsed scene. `GerberViewer` passes mesh scene objects to `Viewer2D` and applies layer visibility, order, and focus. The shared `SceneViewport` owns the zoom and fit toolbar, so standalone `Viewer2D` and `Viewer3D` instances include it too. The toolbar appears when rendering is ready; 3D viewers also include isometric, top, and front presets. Workspaces can append format-specific actions through `extraControls`, which STEP uses for its wireframe toggle. Array order determines planar stacking; clear exposures remain scoped to their own object. Shared viewer and graphics engine modules do not import format adapters.

Both viewers also share viewport sizing, redraw scheduling, zoom buttons, keyboard shortcuts, and wheel delta conversion. The fit button shows the current zoom percentage, with the fitted view at 100%. Buttons, keyboard shortcuts, scrolling, and pinch gestures stay within 25%–1600%; zoom buttons disable at their respective limits. `Viewer2DHandle` exposes reset, zoom, and pan; `Viewer3DHandle` exposes fit, zoom, and isometric/top/front views. The 2D `onChange` callback reports camera state and rendering diagnostics.
